🔍 1. Old vs. Refined McKinsey Capstone Scoring Methodology

As an ISB EPBM Capstone project, maintaining stability across your core scope is essential. The refined methodology retains all 15 charter dimensions and the core 1–4 maturity rubric, but upgrades the scoring engine from rigid integer rounding to continuous precision and evidence-based risk adjustment.

OLD METHODOLOGY Step-Function Integer Engine

  • Intermediate Integer Rounding

    Subdivision averages are forcibly rounded to discrete integers (1, 2, 3, 4), creating artificial performance cliff jumps (e.g., 2.67 → 3.0).

  • Coarse Bucket Midpoints

    Maps level integers to fixed midpoints (Level 1 = 33, Level 2 = 58, Level 3 = 78, Level 4 = 95), treating a weak Level 3 (2.67) identical to a strong Level 3 (3.33).

  • Ignored Confidence Scores

    Confidence (High/Med/Low) is tracked as decorative metadata and never affects the mathematical score.

REFINED MCKINSEY METHOD Continuous & Risk-Adjusted

  • Exact Decimal Maturity (M between 1.00 and 4.00)

    Preserves exact continuous average (M = Average of Subdivision Scores), accurately capturing fractional progress (e.g., 2.67 = Strong Developing).

  • Continuous Linear 0–100 Scaling

    Uses exact linear transformation: Score = 25 + [(M - 1.0) / 3.0] × 75, giving an accurate 0–100 representation.

  • Risk-Adjusted Confidence Discounting

    Applies source-authority multiplier (High = 1.00, Medium = 0.85, Low = 0.70) to calculate Adjusted Score = Nominal Score × Confidence Multiplier.

🗣️ Simple Plain-English Guide (Scoring Explained in Layman's Terms)

Imagine you are evaluating a company's health just like a doctor evaluating a patient's physical fitness. Here is how the old system worked versus how our new simple, fair system works:

🔴 The Old System: The "School Report Card Rounding" Problem

Think of a student who gets A (3.0) in Math, C (2.0) in Science, and A (3.0) in History. Their real average grade is a B (2.67).

The Old Flaw: The system rounded 2.67 up to a flat 3.0 and gave the student an "A" (78%). It hid the fact that the student was struggling in Science!

🟢 The New System: The "Fair Digital Scale" Solution

Instead of rounding, our new scale works like a digital bathroom scale. If you weigh 66.8 kg, it shows exactly 66.8 kg—it doesn't round you up to 78 kg!

The New Fix: The company gets a score of exactly 66.8%. Every small effort to improve counts, and weak areas aren't hidden!

The 3 Everyday Rules of the New Scoring System:

1. No Artificial Jumps

We don't round 2.67 to 3.0. If you are 67% ready, your score shows 67%, not 78%. Small improvements in your company are immediately recognized.

2. Proof Matters (Receipt Check)

If a company claims "We are strong" using official audited tax filings (10-K), they get 100% credit. If they use a random blog post, we apply a "trust discount."

3. Weakest Links Aren't Hidden

If a castle has strong 10-foot walls everywhere but leaves the back door unlocked, it isn't safe. The new score highlights hidden weak spots clearly.

⚡ 3. Why 2×2 "AI vs Macro" Stress Testing Fails

The current stress testing relies on 4 static scenarios: Autonomous Advantage, Storm & Signal, Managed Modernization, Exposed & Reactive.

The Problem: Under "disruptive macro" conditions, the mock/heuristic engine subtracts -1 point uniformly across 11 out of 15 dimensions. This evaluates macroeconomic sentiment, not operational shock resistance.

Real corporate stress testing (such as bank CCAR or McKinsey Resilience Shocks) does not ask "Will the economy be bad?" It injects acute, vector-specific shocks to test structural break-points (e.g., sudden 25% NRR drop or 48-hour active cloud outage).

Pillar 1: Hybrid Min-Weighted Aggregation Formula

Combines weighted average capability with an explicit bottleneck penalty:

Mdimension = α · min(Si) + (1 - α) · ∑ (wi · Si)

Where Si ∈ [1.00, 4.00] are continuous sub-scores, wi are materiality weights (∑ wi = 1.0), and α ≈ 0.30 is the bottleneck penalty factor.
